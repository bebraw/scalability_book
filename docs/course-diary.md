# Keep a course diary in Obsidian

Your course diary gives you one note per lecture.
Keep a prediction, a passage from the book and what you later found out together.
At the end of each phase, choose the entries you want to share in your individual portfolio.
For the diary requirement, submit an idea or a connection from at least seven different lectures across the course.

## Start with the first lecture

Obsidian is a note-taking app.
It saves your writing in text files and calls the folder that holds them a **vault**.
The instructor will show a sample diary in Lecture 1.
After class, use this guide to set up your own diary and write your first prediction.
You can do both without signing in.

The screenshots show Obsidian 1.15.1 on macOS.
Select a screenshot to enlarge it.

### 1. Install Obsidian and unpack the course diary

Download Obsidian for your computer.
On Windows, run the installer.
On macOS, open the downloaded file and drag Obsidian into **Applications**.
On Linux, choose the package for your distribution. [Download Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/download) · [Installation instructions](https://obsidian.md/help/install)

Download the starter files as well.
In your Downloads folder, double-click the ZIP on macOS or right-click it and choose **Extract All** on Windows.
On Linux, use your file manager’s extract action. [Download the course diary (.zip)](/downloads/course-diary.zip)

Move the extracted **Course diary** folder somewhere you can find again, such as **Documents**.
Open it in your file manager and look for **00-Start here** and the **Establish**, **Challenge** and **Compare** folders.
This is the folder you’ll select in Obsidian.
Keep it outside your group’s project repository.

### 2. Open the course diary as a vault

Launch Obsidian.
On the welcome screen, find **Open folder as vault** and click **Open** on that row.
Select the **Course diary** folder you just checked and confirm.

[![Obsidian’s welcome screen with the Open button beside Open folder as vault.](/assets/obsidian-setup/open-vault-1-15-1.png)](/assets/obsidian-setup/open-vault-1-15-1.png)

If Obsidian asks **Do you trust the author of this vault?**, choose **Browse vault in Restricted Mode**.
You can write, follow links and save your first entry in this mode.

[![The first-open trust dialog with Browse vault in Restricted Mode at the lower left.](/assets/obsidian-setup/restricted-mode-1-15-1.png)](/assets/obsidian-setup/restricted-mode-1-15-1.png)

You should see **00-Start here** in the left sidebar.
If Obsidian opened an empty vault or a different folder, click the vault name at the bottom left, choose **Manage vaults**, then repeat **Open folder as vault**. [Manage vaults](https://obsidian.md/help/manage-vaults)

### 3. Write a prediction and find it again

Click **00-Start here**, then follow **Lecture 1**.
You can also expand **Establish** in the left sidebar and click **Lecture 1**.

A team says its cached summary loads twice as fast.
What would you measure before deciding to keep the cache?
If you see a pencil at the top right of the note, click it to edit.
Place your cursor on a new line below the **Reading** link and write one sentence in your own words.

[![The file list shows Establish expanded and Lecture 1 selected. The editor shows where to write a prediction below the Reading link.](/assets/obsidian-setup/first-prediction-1-15-1.png)](/assets/obsidian-setup/first-prediction-1-15-1.png)

Obsidian saves as you type.
Click **00-Start here**, then reopen **Establish → Lecture 1**.
When you see your sentence again, your diary is ready.

Keep this first answer for Lecture 9. If a later observation changes your advice, add a dated note below it.
You can also edit these files in a plain text editor.
For an audio or video diary, add the recording or a link beside the prompt.
Keep a backup of your folder and recordings. [Diary and participation criteria](/project/grading/#participation-and-learning-diary-3-points-per-lecture)

### Use the slides before and after class

In your lecture note, choose **Lecture slides (draft)** to open the current deck.
The Lecture 1 note also links to the **Course welcome and project launch** presentation for grading, group formation and course links.
For later lectures, skim the slides before class and write down one question you want to bring.
After class, return to that question and add one observation that helped you answer it.
For a presentation session, that might be a measurement another team showed.
Choose **Optional self-study** if you want to try an exercise on your own.

The slides are drafts.
Reopening the same link brings up the latest published version.
To save a particular slide on desktop, point at its heading, right-click the **#** that appears and copy the link.
Paste it beside your question in the diary.
If your vault predates these links, choose your lecture from the index and paste its address into your existing note. [All lecture slides](/slides/)

### Read the chapter beside your note (optional)

The **Reading** link in Lecture 1 opens the book.
If it opens in Chrome or another browser, you can keep reading there.
To keep the book and your diary side by side in desktop Obsidian, use its built-in **Web viewer**.

Click the **Settings** gear at the bottom left.
Under **Core plugins**, turn on **Web viewer**.
Then click the small gear on the **Web viewer** row to open its settings.

[![The Web viewer row in Core plugins, showing the settings gear and the enabled switch.](/assets/obsidian-setup/web-viewer-1-15-1.png)](/assets/obsidian-setup/web-viewer-1-15-1.png)

Turn on **Open external links** and close Settings.

[![The Open external links switch turned on in Web viewer settings.](/assets/obsidian-setup/external-links-1-15-1.png)](/assets/obsidian-setup/external-links-1-15-1.png)

Right-click the **Lecture 1** tab and choose **Split right**.
In the right-hand copy, click **Reading**.
You should see the chapter on the right and your prediction on the left. [Obsidian Web viewer](https://obsidian.md/help/plugins/web-viewer)
You can open **Lecture slides (draft)** beside your note the same way.

## Bring a passage into your diary

With the **Course diary** plugin enabled in your desktop vault, select the sentences you want on a book page and choose **Add book highlight** at the bottom of the page.
If your browser asks to open Obsidian, allow it.
Choose the lecture, review the quotation and source link, then choose **Save highlight**. [Enable the course plugin](#use-the-optional-course-plugin)

For manual paste, including in Restricted Mode, choose **Copy highlight** and paste the quote into your lecture entry.
Both actions keep a link back to the passage.
You can save private highlights while signed out.
Under the quote, add why you kept it or what you plan to check.

If you already wrote your initial thinking in Obsidian, select those words in your saved note, right-click and choose **Use as my initial thinking**.
Review the book passage and choose **Open class discussion**.
Only the reference to your saved attempt travels with you.
If you start from the book, open the comment control beside the paragraph, choose **Discuss with classmates** and save your initial thinking with the standalone companion.
In either case, sign in to your course and choose **Reveal peer inquiry** to see threads for that reading.
You can reply or start a thread for your group or the course cohort.

To send feedback privately to the book team, use the same paragraph control.
When sign-in is needed, Obsidian’s desktop Web viewer opens the same paragraph in your default browser.
Sign in with GitHub there, write your feedback and choose **Post comment**.

To see your highlights again when you return to the original web page, you can turn on the Highlighter in Obsidian's optional Web Clipper browser extension.
The extension remembers highlighted passages in your browser and lets you save them to a note. [Web Clipper Highlighter](https://obsidian.md/help/web-clipper/highlight)

## Use the optional course plugin

The starter includes the **Course diary** plugin for desktop Obsidian.
To use it, open **Settings → Community plugins**, choose **Exit Restricted mode** (called **Turn on community plugins** in earlier versions), then enable **Course diary** under **Installed plugins**.

[![The Course diary plugin in Installed plugins, with its switch on at the right.](/assets/obsidian-setup/course-plugin-1-15-1.png)](/assets/obsidian-setup/course-plugin-1-15-1.png)

You can add highlights directly from the book.
To find the commands inside Obsidian, open the command palette with **Ctrl+P** or **Cmd+P** and search for **Course diary**. [Obsidian's community plugin settings](https://obsidian.md/help/community-plugins)

| Command                    | When to use it                                                                                                               |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Open lecture diary         | Jump to the lecture you are studying.                                                                                        |
| Add book highlight         | After copying a highlight in the book, choose its lecture, review the quote and confirm.                                     |
| Discuss with the course TA | Review the excerpt or question you want to bring to the reading's assistant.                                                 |
| Use as my initial thinking | Use a saved selection for the initial step in a class discussion.                                                            |
| Prepare portfolio excerpt  | Select text in a lecture note and review what will go into that phase’s private draft, such as **Portfolio - Establish.md**. |

### Use the lecture folders in an existing vault

Unzip the course diary outside your vault.
Copy **Establish**, **Challenge** and **Compare** into your vault root, keeping any files you already have with the same name.
Copy **00-Start here** and **04-Portfolio** to the vault root for direct links to all nine lectures.

To enable the course commands for the first time, copy the starter’s `.obsidian/plugins/scalableweb-course-diary/` folder into the same location in your vault.
Show hidden files if needed, reopen the vault and enable **Course diary**.
If you already use the plugin, [update it](#update-the-course-plugin-in-an-existing-vault) to use its commands with the new lecture folders.

### Update the course plugin in an existing vault

[Download Course diary plugin 0.4.1 (.zip)](/downloads/course-diary-plugin-0.4.1.zip) and unzip it outside your vault.
Close Obsidian.
In your vault’s `.obsidian/plugins/scalableweb-course-diary/` folder, replace just `main.js` and `manifest.json` with the two files from the download.
Reopen the vault and check the version of **Course diary** under **Settings → Community plugins**.

## Discuss a question with the TA

With the desktop plugin, select a few lines of your diary, right-click and choose **Discuss with the course TA**.
Review the excerpt and add your question, for example, “Would my measurement catch slow cache misses?”
Choose **Use Obsidian TA** to run the assistant on this computer while the vault is open.
The first time, enter the ID of a Chat Completions model available to your account and your OpenAI API key.
The plugin uses Obsidian’s secret storage for the key.
Review the reading address and choose **Start TA and open reading**.
Use that reading in Chrome on the same computer.
Choose **Connect to Obsidian** and allow local network access if asked.
Paste the reviewed text and choose **Send message** when ready.
Your provider bills API usage.
You can choose **Stop Obsidian TA** in the command palette when finished.
The same vault and book website reopen their previous TA conversations on this computer.
Moving the vault or switching between a preview and the published site opens a separate conversation history.

If you use another editor, prefer another provider or want to reopen conversations saved with the standalone companion, use [the standalone setup](/notebook/setup/#start-on-your-computer).
Open the reading in Chrome on the companion’s computer and choose **Teaching assistant → Discuss**.
The plugin’s **Copy and open beside diary** and **Copy and open in default browser** actions also work with your existing companion.

Back in your lecture entry, record which part of your explanation changed and what you will try next.
Identify any assistant wording you keep.

## Prepare material for your portfolio

1. Open **04-Portfolio** and choose a phase.
   Its table maps each phase’s three portfolio entries to the course lecture numbers shown in the workspace.
   Select a passage or recording from each entry you want to include.
   Check what the phase requires for participation, self-assessment, feedback and a decision you influenced. [Assessment rubric](/project/grading/)
2. Sign in to the [course workspace](/course/), select your course and phase, then open **My portfolio**.
   Paste your chosen excerpts into the matching fields.
   For written, audio or video files, choose **Attach written, audio or video evidence**, select the uploaded attachment and explain its relevance.
   Use links that staff can open outside your vault.
3. Check the material and choose **Submit portfolio** by the announced deadline.
   Check the displayed status. **Save draft** leaves the portfolio in draft.
   Course staff can access saved drafts and uploaded files.
   Keep private entries and confidential peer feedback out of the group's shared technical submission.

## Work with your project group in Obsidian

Each teammate can open their own copy of the group's private Git repository as a second vault.
In Obsidian choose **Open folder as vault**, select the project root containing `projects/` and open **README → Work together in Obsidian**. [Download the three-project starter](/downloads/project/project-starter.zip)

[![The group project vault, with the Work together in Obsidian guide open and the Challenge evidence index visible in the file list.](/assets/obsidian-setup/group-project-1-15-1.png)](/assets/obsidian-setup/group-project-1-15-1.png)

The group can use the same vault for Establish, Challenge and Compare while building each implementation in its own directory.
During an engineering session, record what the team tested and decided in its decision record or AI-use log.
Link that record from the current phase’s evidence index so a teammate can pull the changes and repeat the check.
Keep your individual reflections and peer assessments in your personal diary. [Shared engineering sessions](/project/#shared-engineering-sessions)

## Bring over an existing Notebook

If you wrote in the book's earlier Notebook, open [Notebook recovery](/notebook/setup/?legacy=1) in Chrome with your original companion running.
Choose **Connect companion** and then **Download previous notes as Markdown**.
Move the file into your vault.
It contains the latest text of every entry and its source link.
You can copy passages from that file into the matching lecture notes.

For a private backup with earlier revisions and concept judgments, choose **Export notebook backup** and keep the JSON file. [Companion backup and transfer](/notebook/setup/#keep-your-work-portable)
