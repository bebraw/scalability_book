The demonstration establishes the checked export behavior on synthetic data. A first trial can stay in the sandbox and test an authorized workspace, rejection of another workspace, and the exact exported fields. Supplying a workspace identifier is not evidence of permission: the application must enforce the reader's access independently of the interface. A later live trial needs explicit data and deployment authority, checks on the actual configuration, and an owner able to stop the trial and restore the previous reporting path. The current sandbox permission does not grant that transition.

Disabling the faulty export path can stop new exports but cannot retract a file already copied elsewhere. The owner must also address the affected data under the applicable incident process. Agree on a bounded observation period and compare accepted reports and time saved with review, corrections, and support effort, including unfinished work. Expansion depends on those outcomes and the agreed access rules; the ability to generate the tool alone does not settle whether it is useful to operate.
