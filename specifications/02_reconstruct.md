# Reconstruct Project

## Scaling an Event Tracking System

---

## 1. Purpose of the Reconstruct Project

In this project, you will **revisit and redesign** your previous Event Tracking Application with a new primary concern: **scalability**.

Rather than building a completely new product, you are asked to **reconstruct the system conceptually and technically** to handle significantly increased scale, complexity, and uncertainty.

The focus is on **architectural reasoning**, not on implementing every detail.

---

## 2. Starting Point

You should assume that:

- Your original system works correctly for a small instance of the chosen domain
- The core concepts (events, time, and domain context) still apply
- The original implementation is insufficient for large-scale usage

You may reuse ideas, code, or models from the first project, but you are not required to.

---

## 3. New Assumptions (Changed Requirements)

The specification is intentionally changed as follows:

- The system must support **many concurrent tenants, projects, groups, locations, or other domain instances**
- Events may be generated by **tens or hundreds of thousands of users**
- Event volume is **high and bursty**
- Failures (network, services, delays) are expected
- Real-time accuracy is less important than **eventual correctness**

You do NOT need to implement all of this fully.

---

## 4. Core Task (Mandatory)

You MUST produce a **scalability-aware redesign** of your system.

This includes:

### 4.1 Architecture Proposal

- A high-level system architecture
- Identification of major components
- Description of responsibilities and boundaries

### 4.2 Event Flow

- How events enter the system
- How they are processed and stored
- How they are queried or summarized

Diagrams are strongly encouraged.

---

## 5. Scalability Considerations (Must Address)

Your design MUST explicitly discuss how it handles:

- Increased event volume
- Concurrent users
- Storage growth over time
- Partial failures or retries

You may choose the techniques used.

---

## 6. Optional Implementation Work

You MAY implement parts of your redesigned system.

Examples include:

- A revised ingestion API
- A message queue or buffer
- A partitioned or append-only storage model
- A read-optimized query path

Partial implementations are acceptable if well explained.

---

## 7. Explicit Trade-offs

You MUST describe trade-offs you are making, such as:

- Simplicity vs scalability
- Consistency vs availability
- Real-time results vs batch processing
- Resource efficiency vs convenience, speed, or operational safety

There are no universally correct answers.

---

## 8. Deliverables

Your submission SHALL include:

- A short design document (5–10 pages or equivalent)
- Architecture and data-flow diagrams
- A written explanation of scalability decisions
- Optional: supporting code or prototypes

---

## 9. Evaluation Criteria

You will be evaluated on:

- Quality of architectural reasoning
- Understanding of scalability challenges
- Clarity of communication
- Appropriate abstraction level
- Honest and well-justified trade-offs

Implementation completeness is less important than design quality.

---

## 10. Guidance

Think in terms of **pressure**:

- What breaks first?
- What can be delayed?
- What can be duplicated?

The goal is not to predict the future perfectly, but to design systems that fail gracefully.
